Meet Gappu

Gappu has big ears, a yellow cap and an even bigger heart. He isn’t perfect — he grabs, he bumps, he forgets. Then he stops, thinks, and tries again. That’s exactly why children see themselves in him.

Big ears

Gappu is learning to listen — to Amma, to his friends, and to the little voice that says “wait!”

Yellow cap

A present from Dadi. He never takes it off, so you can always spot him — even in a crowd of elephants.

Curly trunk

His trunk-wave is how he says thank you. Kids love copying it — try it at the dinner table!

“Oops! Gappu-sorry!”

— what Gappu says every time he gets it wrong

For parents & teachers

Why a little elephant works better than “say thank you!” for the hundredth time.

1

Kids copy characters

A lecture is forgotten by lunchtime. A friend who gets it wrong and then makes it right is remembered — and imitated.

2

One manner per book

Each story teaches just one thing, with the words repeated so little ones can say them along with you.

3

Talk-about-it pages

Every book ends with simple questions and a spot-the-good-manners activity, so the lesson moves from the page into real life.
